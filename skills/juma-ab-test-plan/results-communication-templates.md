# Results Communication Templates

Use these templates to communicate A/B test results to non-technical client stakeholders. Choose the appropriate template based on the test outcome.

## Win Template

> "We tested [change] against the original [page/element]. After [N] visitors over [N] weeks, the new version increased [metric] by [X%], which translates to approximately [Y] additional [conversions/revenue] per month. We recommend implementing this change permanently."

### Example:
> "We tested a shorter lead form (4 fields instead of 8) against the original on the demo request page. After 28,000 visitors over 3 weeks, the shorter form increased completion rate by 18%, which translates to approximately 45 additional demo requests per month. We recommend implementing the shorter form permanently."

## Loss Template

> "We tested [change] against the original [page/element]. After [N] visitors over [N] weeks, the original performed better. This is still a valuable result -- we now know [what we learned] and can apply this insight to [next test]. Our next test will focus on [next hypothesis]."

### Example:
> "We tested adding a video testimonial above the fold on the pricing page against the original static layout. After 15,000 visitors over 4 weeks, the original page performed better by 7%. This is still a valuable result -- we now know that adding media above the fold may distract visitors from the pricing comparison. Our next test will focus on placing the testimonial below the pricing table where it can address concerns after the visitor has reviewed options."

## Inconclusive Template

> "We tested [change] over [N] weeks but didn't see a statistically meaningful difference. This means [change] likely doesn't have a large impact on [metric]. We recommend [next action: extend test, try bigger change, move to next test]."

### Example:
> "We tested a green CTA button versus the current blue CTA button over 4 weeks but didn't see a statistically meaningful difference in click-through rate. This means button color likely doesn't have a large impact on conversions for this page. We recommend moving to our next test, which focuses on the CTA copy itself -- a variable more likely to influence visitor behavior."

## Tips for Client Communication

- **Lead with the business impact**, not the statistical details
- **Translate percentages into real numbers** (conversions, revenue, leads) that the client cares about
- **Frame losses and inconclusives as learnings**, not failures -- every test generates knowledge
- **Always include the next step** so the client sees testing as an ongoing optimization process, not a one-time event
- **Avoid jargon**: Do not use "p-value," "statistical significance," or "confidence interval" in client-facing communication unless the client has requested technical detail
